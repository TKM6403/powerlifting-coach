# Decision Log: 2026-06-09 — Belt Squat as Primary Lower Movement + Return-to-Barbell Ladder

**Trigger:** During the 6/9 remote-control session (Wk 3/4 Reintro Accumulation), after the loaded-hinge debut reproduced a faint L-calf nerve tickle (the named stop signal; faded; athlete overrode the stop and finished the set), tkm voiced real frustration and fear of losing leg muscle, and a strong pull to deadlift/squat heavy again. He proposed a safe pushable lower movement (belt squat) and asked for the explicit path back to the barbell + what externals he controls. Athlete-initiated; buy-in obtained before writing.

**Agents consulted:** SBD (lead), Mobility, Aesthetics. Logging Agent wrote the session log separately (`logs/sessions/2026-06-09.md`).

## Decision (Head Coach synthesis)

1. **Belt squat replaces the Friday depth-limited leg press as the primary quad/glute movement.** 3×10–12, RPE 8 last set (can push near failure — spine-unloaded, fatigue-cheap), controlled 2–3 sec eccentric. **Depth-limited to ~parallel with a physical hard stop** (out of the R-quad deep-flexion bottom). Light feeler set first, ramp load only on clean reads both sides. Optional single-leg/split belt-squat finisher (L-first) to attack the L-glute lag; trim-first.
2. **Gated on a clean 6/10 AM L-leg read** (Mobility condition). If the leg barks Wed–Fri, belt squat holds too and we talk first.
3. **Return-to-barbell ladder formalized** (two independent ladders, squat + deadlift), each rung gated on a clean read *in-session AND next morning*. Written into `program/this-week.md` ("Your path back to the barbell").
4. **Controllables** the athlete committed to, written into the plan: daily resets, standing desk/anti-sitting, sleep→7.5–8h, daily L-glute work, morning-after check.

## Agent positions

### SBD Agent (lead)
- Belt squat earns its slot on first principles: spine-unloaded axial squat pattern (no lumbar/SI load — key for the L nerve), depth trivially cappable (keeps R quad out of the deep-flexion bottom), hypertrophy-honest (answers the atrophy fear). Sources: SBS/Nuckols + Meadows/Bryant (belt squat as spine-sparing high-volume quad builder); RP/Israetel + SBS double-progression for progression logic.
- Built both ladders with explicit gates. Squat ladder favors front-loaded/heel-elevated re-entry (less asymmetric, less SI-provoking than low-bar). Deadlift ladder: light RDL → trap bar → conventional; **sumo stays parked** (demands L hip extension + IR, historical flare point) despite the higher sumo number.
- **Pre-conceded the disagreement:** after 6/9, agreed a single clean light-RDL read is thin — endorsed requiring **two consecutive clean loaded-hinge reads** before trap bar.
- Flagged that the **stop-signal override is the real risk** — the ladder's gates are meaningless if he trains through stops.

### Mobility Agent
- **Supports belt squat, GATED on the 6/10 AM read.** Belt squat is mechanically the wrong shape to provoke the L nerve (no spinal flexion/bend-over, vertical trunk, load tractions from the hip) — it *sidesteps* the gate rather than testing it; that's the feature. Source: belt squat is the standard spine-decoupled squat in S&C/rehab (EliteFTS/Westside; clinical return-to-squat).
- **Binding constraint is the R quad, not the nerve:** full-depth belt squat = loaded deep flexion = the R-quad provoking position. Depth capped to ~parallel, physical hard stop required (fatigue/ego can't sink past it). Light + ramping.
- Keep existing warmup as-is (already correct); add one light belt-squat ramp set so he doesn't load cold (the cold-start pattern strained the R quad on 6/2).
- Controllables ranked: standing desk/anti-sitting likely does the most for the timeline (sitting is the documented re-trigger); daily resets #1 protective dose; sleep; morning-after check; daily L-glute activation. **The single most timeline-positive behavior is honoring the stop signal instead of overriding it.**
- Red flags that cannot be overridden: hard leg drive/arch on bench stay HELD; no full-depth belt squat; depth hard-stop in place.

### Aesthetics Agent
- **Reassurance is accurate:** maintenance needs ~1/3 to 1/9 of growth volume and holds for months (Bickel 2011: gains held on ~1/9 volume for 32 wks; RP/Israetel MV ≈ ≤6 sets/wk; SBS "easy to maintain, hard to build"). The squat/DL hold is *reduced* loading, not disuse — atrophy is slow. Current safe-leg menu **maintains quads/glutes and is actively growing hamstrings** (~5 curl sets/wk = at MEV).
- Belt squat hypertrophy scheme: 3×10–15, last set to RPE 8–9/near-failure (safe to grind — no spinal/CNS cost), 2–3 sec eccentric, depth at/above parallel until R quad clears. Hypertrophy lever = reps + proximity to failure + ROM, not load — ideal for a can't-go-heavy phase. Source: Nippard/RP on stable movements letting you push closer to failure safely.
- Proposed exactly ONE lean addition (single-leg belt squat, L-first) for the L-glute deficit; explicitly declined to bloat (no extra calves, no leg-ext, no adductor machines — daily routine covers it).

## Disagreement (surfaced to athlete per hard rule)

**SBD ↔ Mobility, same axis as the 6/7 trap-bar call.** SBD's instinct is to climb clean rungs fast (aggressive volume in accumulation). Mobility caps belt-squat depth/load until the R-quad clears, and wants **two** consecutive clean light-RDL reads before trap bar (not one). SBD pre-conceded. **Head Coach sided with Mobility (two-read rule)** — the 6/9 reproduction + the repeated stop-signal overrides (5/26, 6/9) make a single in-session "clean" read unreliable. Tradeoff shown to tkm: slightly slower return vs. not re-lighting a nerve we've now reproduced under load.
