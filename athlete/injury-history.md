## Working Hypothesis: Left AIC Pattern

Based on cumulative symptoms and self-assessment, tkm presents a pattern strongly consistent with **left anteriorly rotated innominate** (left AIC / PRI left AIC pattern). This has not been formally diagnosed by a PT — strongly recommended to pursue this for confirmation and direction-specific corrections.

### Cardinal Findings

| Finding | Status |
|---|---|
| Anterior pelvic tilt, worse on left | Confirmed self-assessment |
| Left hip flexor tight, overactive | Confirmed (responds to couch stretch) |
| Left glute weak, underactive | Confirmed ("can't feel it firing") |
| Left hip internal rotation limited | Originally severe; improved with targeted work |
| Asymmetric forward hinge (right hand drops lower) | Confirmed self-test |
| Recurring left-side injuries per training block | Pattern across multiple blocks |
| Right adductor/high hamstring fires when left is consciously loaded | Observed during recent squat session |

### Mechanism (As Best Understood)

The left half of the pelvis sits tipped forward and slightly down compared to the right. As a result:
- Left hip flexor stays short → tight, feels chronically restricted
- Left glute is lengthened → mechanically disadvantaged, hard to recruit
- Left femur sits in a position limiting IR
- Left SI joint takes asymmetric stress under load
- Right side compensates for force production and stability
- When the athlete consciously over-corrects toward the left (e.g., "push through the left in squat"), the right side scrambles to stabilize and previously-quiet structures (right adductor, right high hamstring origin) light up

## What Has Helped

- **Couch stretch (left)** with proper pelvic tuck and glute squeeze — 60-90 sec holds
- **Single-leg glute bridges** (left leg planted) — wakes up left glute progressively
- **Banded clamshells** — basic external rotation and glute med activation
- **Banded internal rotation work** — addresses the IR deficit specifically
- **Daily stabilizer routine** done before lifting or on rest days
- **Symmetric setup cues** on squat ("both feet drive equally") rather than over-corrective ("push left harder")
- **Wide-grip / slightly-flared elbow bench** — eases left elbow symptoms that flare with strict tuck

## What Has NOT Worked or Has Backfired

- **Aggressive unilateral over-correction on squat** ("push hard through left") — caused right-side adductor and high hamstring flare
- **Generic "stretch what's tight" advice** — incomplete; the pattern needs position correction, not just stretching
- **Skipping stabilizer work during heavy SBD blocks** — guaranteed new injury
- **Rounding the upper back on the unrack to "neutralize the pelvis" under SSB (2026-05-26, self-improvised, NOT prescribed)** — athlete attempted to counter anterior pelvic tilt by intentionally flexing the thoracic spine on unrack and pre-descent under load. This introduces spinal flexion under axial compression without addressing the actual pelvis mechanism (left AIC / left innominate position). It does not correct the pelvis — it just adds a flexion moment to the loaded spine. Symptom progressed during the attempt (posterior L hamstring shoot reproduced under ~95-115 lb SSB load). **Anti-cue. Do not revisit. Do not add to `reference/coaching-cues.md`.** Pelvic-position correction belongs in pre-lift PRI/breathing work, not in a flexed-thoracic unrack cue under the bar.
