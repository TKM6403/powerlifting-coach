# Daily Stabilizer Routine

This is the routine referenced in `program/this-week.md` as "daily stabilizer routine" on rest days (and during ankle-recovery substitution).

It is intentionally **anti-extension, anti-rotation, and lateral-chain biased** — NOT crunches or sit-ups. Those would reinforce the AIC pattern (overactive L hip flexor and rectus abdominis dominance) we're trying to walk back from. This routine does the opposite.

---

## Why this routine for tkm specifically

Your pattern (left AIC / anterior pelvic tilt, overactive L hip flexor, weak L glute, limited L hip internal rotation, R-side compensation) needs:

- **Anti-extension core** (dead bug, bird dog) — teaches your low back to stay neutral instead of arching into extension under load.
- **Anti-rotation core** (Pallof) — teaches your obliques and transverse abdominis to resist twisting forces. Underdosed in most lifters; especially important for someone with rotational asymmetry.
- **Glute reactivation** (single-leg glute bridge, clamshell) — wakes up the underactive L glute progressively, without forcing the conscious "push the left harder" cue that has backfired before.
- **Lateral chain** (Copenhagen, clamshell) — addresses L lateral-chain weakness.

What this routine does NOT include and why:
- **No crunches, sit-ups, leg raises, weighted ab wheel** — they load active hip flexion + spinal flexion, which reinforces L psoas/rectus dominance.
- **No heavy back extensions (good morning, GHR to fatigue)** — your low back is already extension-biased from the APT.

---

## When to do it

- **Rest days: Mon, Thu, Sat** (or any rest day if schedule shifts).
- **2 rounds, ~15-20 min total.**
- **Not on lift days** unless explicitly programmed — keeps lift days lean.

---

## The moves (in order)

### 1. Dead bug

**What it is:** Anti-extension core. Teaches your low back to stay pressed into the floor while your arms and legs move.

**How to do it:**
1. Lie on your back on the floor.
2. Arms straight up toward the ceiling (like you're holding up an invisible bar).
3. Knees bent at 90°, shins parallel to the floor (feet up off the ground).
4. Exhale fully, press your lower back HARD into the floor. The gap between your lower back and the floor should close completely.
5. Keeping your lower back pinned to the floor, slowly lower your RIGHT arm overhead AND your LEFT leg straight out at the same time. Lower until your heel is just above the floor.
6. If your lower back starts to arch off the floor — STOP. That's the limit of your range. Don't go past it.
7. Return to start. Switch sides (L arm + R leg).

**Sets × reps:** 2 × 8 per side, slow (2-3 sec each rep).

**Cue:** "Belly button stays glued to spine. Lower back stays glued to floor. Move slow."

---

### 2. Banded clamshell (L side first)

**What it is:** Glute medius activation — wakes up the side of your hip/glute, which is part of your weak L glute pattern.

**How to do it:**
1. Lie on your side, knees stacked and bent at ~45° (like you're sitting in a chair on your side).
2. Loop a mini-band around both legs, just above the knees.
3. Keep your feet stacked and TOGETHER throughout the move.
4. Open your TOP knee up toward the ceiling like a clamshell opening. Keep your feet touching.
5. **Don't let your hips roll backward** as your knee opens. The motion is at the hip joint only.
6. Slow down on the way back to start.

**Sets × reps:** 2 × 12 per side. **Start with the L side** (your weaker side — gets the freshest neural effort).

**Cue:** "Feel it on the OUTSIDE of your hip, not in your low back or front of hip."

---

### 3. Single-leg glute bridge (L leg first)

**What it is:** Direct L glute reactivation.

**How to do it:**
1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor.
2. Lift your RIGHT leg off the ground — either extend it straight out OR hold the knee bent and lifted off the floor.
3. Drive through your LEFT (planted) heel and lift your hips off the ground.
4. Lift until your body is in a straight line from shoulder to knee (don't hyperextend past flat).
5. **Squeeze your LEFT glute HARD** at the top — 1-2 sec hold.
6. Lower slowly. Repeat.

**Sets × reps:** 2 × 8 per leg. **Start with the L leg planted** (the side that needs the work most).

**Cue:** "Squeeze the L glute like you're trying to crack a walnut between your cheeks at the top. Don't let your low back arch — the lift comes from the glute, not the back."

---

### 4. Bird dog

**What it is:** Anti-rotation + anti-extension core, with bonus shoulder/hip stability.

**How to do it:**
1. Hands and knees position. Hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
2. Brace your core — ribs down, back flat (not sagging, not rounded).
3. At the same time, reach your RIGHT arm STRAIGHT FORWARD and your LEFT leg STRAIGHT BACK. Both arms and leg should end up parallel to the floor.
4. **Keep your hips square** — don't let them tilt as your leg goes back. Imagine balancing a glass of water on your low back.
5. Hold 1-2 sec at the top. Return. Switch sides (L arm + R leg).

**Sets × reps:** 2 × 8 per side.

**Cue:** "Hips square. No twisting. Move slow."

---

### 5. Pallof press

**What it is:** Pure anti-rotation core. Trains your obliques and deep core to resist a sideways pulling force.

**How to do it:**
1. Set up a cable machine handle at chest height. Alternatively: anchor a resistance band to a sturdy upright at chest height (door handle works in a pinch with a door anchor).
2. Stand SIDEWAYS to the cable — so the cable is pulling toward one of your sides (perpendicular to the front of your body).
3. Grab the handle with BOTH hands, hold it at your sternum.
4. Step out to a distance where there's tension on the cable.
5. **Press the handle STRAIGHT OUT** in front of you until your arms are fully extended (away from the cable, NOT to the side).
6. The cable will try to twist your torso TOWARD the anchor. **Don't let it.** Keep your shoulders square, hips facing straight ahead.
7. Hold the extended position for 1-2 sec.
8. Slowly bring the handle back to your sternum.

**Sets × reps:** 2 × 10 per side. Do both sides (cable on your L, then cable on your R).

**Cue:** "The cable wants to rotate you. You don't let it. Your job is to NOT move."

**Load:** light to moderate — enough that you have to actively resist the pull. Too heavy = you'll lean to compensate. Too light = no challenge.

---

### 6. Regressed Copenhagen (L leg working first)

**What it is:** Adductor (inner thigh) + lateral core. Critical for someone with your L hip pattern — the L adductor has been part of the compensation picture.

**How to do it (REGRESSED version — start here):**
1. Lie on your side, propped up on your bottom forearm and elbow (forearm flat on the floor, elbow under shoulder, like a forearm side plank setup).
2. Place your TOP leg's KNEE on a bench (so your top leg is supported at the knee, NOT the foot). Your top shin and knee rest on the bench surface.
3. Your bottom leg is straight and HOVERING just below the bench level — not touching the floor, not touching the bench.
4. **Hold this position.** Your inner thigh of the TOP leg should be working isometrically to keep you stable. Your bottom-side obliques should also fire.
5. Don't sag — keep your hips lifted, body in a straight line from shoulders to knees.

**Sets × duration:** 2 × 15-30 sec hold per side. Start at 15 sec, progress to 30 sec as it gets easier. Start with **L leg in the TOP/working position** first.

**Cue:** "Feel it on the INSIDE of your top thigh. If you feel it in your low back, raise your hips."

**Progression (don't do this version yet — note for future):** "Full Copenhagen" puts the top leg's FOOT (not knee) on the bench, which is dramatically harder. Stay on the regressed version until 30 sec × 2 sides feels easy, then we progress.

## Total time

~15-20 min for 2 rounds. If you're rushed, do 1 round — better than skipping. The minimum effective dose here is one good round done with intent.

---

## Substitutions for ankle-recovery week

During an acute lower-extremity injury (e.g., L ankle sprain) where standing balance is compromised:

- **Skip single-leg glute bridge** (planted L foot uncomfortable) — sub `lying knee abduction with band` (L leg only, lying on back, knees bent, band around knees, press knee out against band — short ROM, no foot demand).
- **Skip Pallof press standing** — sub `tall-kneeling Pallof press` (kneel on both knees on a pad, do the same anti-rotation press; no ankle demand).
- **All other moves** are floor-based and unaffected by ankle status.
