# Coaching Cues

Form cues, setup notes, and form-troubleshooting collected over time. Organized by lift.

---

## Universal Setup Cues

- **Symmetric setup is non-negotiable.** Before any unrack or first rep, look down and verify feet are mirrored. Most asymmetry starts at setup.
- **Brace before you move.** No initiation of any heavy lift without a deliberate brace.
- **"Ribs over pelvis."** Anti-rib-flare cue. Reduces lumbar extension and protects the lower back across all lifts.

---

## Squat

### Setup
- Bar position: low bar (default; adjust if shoulder mobility doesn't allow)
- Stance: shoulder-width to slightly wider, toes 10-30° out
- Feet exactly mirrored
- Air in the belly before unrack, brace into the belt

### Descent
- "Knees out, knees out" — bilaterally
- "Sit between the hips" (not back, not down)
- Maintain bracing the whole way down

### Ascent
- "Push the floor away with both heels at the same time"
- Don't lead with the strong side (right) — this is critical for tkm
- If you feel weight shift to one side: that's a form rep, reset

### Form troubleshooting
- **Left knee caves / left hip shifts backward in the hole** → hip flexor / glute med deficit on the left. Address with mobility, don't grind through.
- **Loss of upper back tightness** → re-cue lat engagement, wedge into the bar harder on setup.
- **Hip rise faster than chest** → too much hip hinge, not enough quad. Or, too heavy.

### Asymmetry-specific note
- Don't deliberately "push harder through the left." That over-corrects and fires up the right adductor/high hamstring. Instead: "both sides drive equally."

## Bench Press

### Setup
- Eyes under bar (or slightly behind), depending on arch
- Arch from T-spine (upper back), NOT from lumbar
- Ribs down, even with the arch
- Scaps retracted AND depressed (down and back) before unrack
- Feet planted, toes pointed slightly out, light leg drive engaged before unrack

### Descent
- Bar path: slight diagonal, lower to mid-chest or below
- Tucked elbows generally — but for tkm, slight elbow flare (45-60° from torso) eases left elbow pain. Use slightly wider grip to match.
- Don't lose scap position on the descent

### Ascent
- Drive the heels into the floor — but down, not out (less adductor demand)
- Glutes squeezed for tightness, not explosive hip drive
- Press up and slightly back toward the rack

### Form troubleshooting
- **Left shoulder / pec minor pain** → scap is protracted or elevated under load. Re-cue retraction and depression. Tighten upper back on setup.
- **Butt comes off bench** → too much vertical leg drive. Drive horizontally / into the floor, not up.
- **Reps grinding by 8-10 on a high-rep set** → that's where form breaks. Stop set if it gets sharp on the left.

### Setup for incline bench
- Don't actively tuck the pelvis. Let the natural lumbar lordosis persist.
- "Ribs down, butt down, chest up."
- Don't try for a flat-bench-style arch — the bench angle eats it.

## Deadlift (Conventional)

### Setup
- Bar over mid-foot
- Hips higher than knees, shoulders slightly in front of bar
- Lats engaged ("squeeze oranges in your armpits")
- Pull slack out of the bar before initiating

### Pull
- Push the floor away
- Knees and hips extend together
- Lock out by squeezing glutes, not hyperextending the lower back

### Form troubleshooting
- **Hips shoot up first** → losing lat tension; reset and re-engage lats.
- **Lower back rounds early** → bar too far forward, or hips too low at start.

## Sumo Deadlift

### Setup
- Wide stance (foot placement depends on hip structure)
- Toes pointed out, knees track over toes
- Hips closer to bar than conventional
- Chest up, vertical torso
- Active external rotation: "screw your feet into the floor"

### Pull
- Drive the floor apart, not just push down
- Knees track out the whole way up
- Lock out by squeezing glutes

### Asymmetry-specific note
- The left hip extension + IR + ER demand is exactly where tkm's pattern shows up. If the left hip feels locked, the entire sumo pull will be compromised. Address mobility first.
- Paused sumo amplifies positional weakness — own the pause before adding weight.

## Form Cues Specific to tkm's Pattern

### To wake up the left glute on any lift
- "Tuck the pelvis under" or "belt buckle to chin" (posterior tilt cues)
- "Squeeze a walnut between your cheeks"
- Posterior pelvic tilt mechanically engages the glutes. If you can't feel the glute squeeze, focus on the tilt cue and the squeeze follows.

### To find symmetric drive on squat
- "Both feet at the same time, same pressure"
- "Drive evenly through both heels"
- Mental check at the bottom: which foot is heavier? Equalize before driving up.

### To stabilize the left shoulder on bench
- "Pull your scaps down and back AND hold them there through every rep"
- "Don't let the left shoulder roll forward at the bottom"

## Diagnostic Self-Tests

### Iliac crest height check (asymmetry)
Stand barefoot, feet hip-width, weight even, eyes closed. Have someone place fingers on iliac crests from behind. Compare height.

### PSIS check (more sensitive)
Same position; partner palpates the back dimples (PSIS). Compare height.

### Standing flexion test
Partner places thumbs on PSIS. Bend forward slowly. If one PSIS rises higher / earlier than the other, that side's SI is restricted.

### Sit vs. stand iliac crest comparison
Asymmetry standing → could be leg length or pelvis. Same asymmetry sitting → pelvis-driven. Asymmetry resolves when seated → leg length issue.

### Supine leg length check
Lie flat, relaxed. Partner compares inner ankle bone heights.

### Hinge test
Stand, fold forward. Are your hands at the same height? Right lower than left = pelvic rotation pattern (consistent with left AIC).

### Single-leg balance check
Stand on one leg, lift other foot. Watch pelvis from behind. Hip drop on lifted side = standing-leg glute med weakness.

### Single-leg glute bridge feel
Best of both: a test AND an exercise. Do bridges on each side. Can you feel the working glute? Compare sides. Improvement over time = the activation work is working.
