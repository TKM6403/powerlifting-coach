# Exercise Substitutions

When you can't or shouldn't do the prescribed lift, here's what to substitute and why.

---

## Travel / Hotel Gym

| Prescribed | Substitute |
|---|---|
| Back squat | Goblet squat, leg press (machine), DB front squat, Bulgarian split squat |
| Bench press | DB bench, machine chest press, push-ups with elevation, dips |
| Deadlift | DB Romanian deadlift, single-leg DB RDL, hip thrust on bench |
| Sumo deadlift | KB sumo deadlift, sumo stance DB RDL |
| Barbell row | DB row, machine row, cable row, inverted row |
| OHP | DB shoulder press, machine shoulder press, landmine press |

### Travel session template
- 5 min full-body warmup (from `warmup-library.md` travel section)
- 1 squat pattern: 3×10
- 1 push: 3×10-15
- 1 pull: 3×10-15
- 1 hinge: 3×10
- 1-2 accessories for pump/maintenance
- Cooldown

Keep RPE 6-7. Maintain, don't try to grow on the road.

---

## When Left Side Is Flared

| Lift | Substitute (Lower Asymmetry) |
|---|---|
| Sumo deadlift | Conventional deadlift (less hip ER demand) |
| Heavy back squat | Front squat (more symmetric loading, less low back) |
| Wide-stance squat | Closer-stance squat |
| Heavy bench (tucked) | Bench with slight elbow flare |
| Conventional deadlift if low back is the issue | Trap bar deadlift, block pull from high pins |

### General principle
- More symmetric variations beat asymmetric ones when a flare is active
- Lower CNS demand beats higher when recovery is compromised
- Range of motion can be reduced (block pulls, board press) to load the rest of the body while sparing the affected area

## When Specific Areas Are Sore (Day-After Modifications)

### Sore glutes
- OK to bench, OK to upper body work
- Squats and deads: reduce weight 5-10%, full warmup mandatory
- Skip aggressive glute activation in warmup (it's already had it)

### Sore adductors
- Narrow stance on squat
- Avoid wide-stance work (sumo, wide-stance squat, sumo squat)
- No Copenhagen plank that day

### Sore hamstrings
- OK to bench, OK to upper accessories
- No RDL / hinge work that day
- Squat: shift to higher bar position (more quad-dominant), reduce depth slightly if needed

### Sore lower back
- No deadlift or heavy squat that day
- Walk, do light upper, do mobility
- If persistent: red flag, escalate

### Sore upper back / lats
- OK to squat, OK to deadlift (counterintuitive but lats stabilize, they're not prime movers)
- Reduce pulling volume that day
- Bench: be extra deliberate about upper back tightness on setup

### Sore shoulders / pec minor
- No heavy bench or OHP
- Substitute machine press or floor press (limits ROM where shoulder is irritated)
- Address with mobility work, escalate if persistent

## When Pain (Not Just Soreness) Is Present

Default to reducing rather than substituting. If a substitute is required:

| Pain Location | Better Choice |
|---|---|
| Left hip (anterior) | Front squat, trap bar deadlift |
| Left SI joint | Front squat, trap bar, avoid sumo |
| Left shoulder anterior | Floor press, neutral-grip DB press |
| Left elbow | Slightly wider grip, slight elbow flare, machine press |
| Lower back | Belt squat, leg press, no deadlift |
| Knee | High bar squat with shorter ROM, avoid lunges, leg extension at moderate load only |

If pain is sharp or sustained over multiple sessions: stop trying to substitute, get assessed.

## When Equipment Is Limited

### No barbell
- Use heaviest DBs available, do higher reps
- Add tempo (3-sec eccentric, 2-sec pause) to make light weights harder
- Add unilateral variations (DBs let you load one side at a time)

### No bench
- Floor press
- Push-ups with feet elevated (decline)
- Push-ups with weight on back

### No squat rack
- Goblet squat (heavy KB or DB held at chest)
- Front-loaded reverse lunge
- Single-leg work

### No pulling implement
- DB rows, single-arm rows
- Inverted rows under a sturdy table
- Towel rows on a fixed object

---

## Aesthetics Substitutions (Keeping Volume When Main Lift Is Out)

When SBD must be regressed, accessories can often continue. Mobility Agent and Aesthetics Agent should coordinate:

- Squat compromised → can usually still do leg press, leg extension, leg curl, hip thrust
- Bench compromised → can usually still do machine press, fly variations, tricep work
- Deadlift compromised → can usually still do hip thrust, back extension (with caution), lat work

The accessory work preserves muscle mass while the main lift recovers. Don't let injury take you out of the gym entirely unless rest is the actual prescription.

---

## Notes

- The right substitution depends on context — what's sore, what equipment is available, what you've been doing recently
- Head Coach should always ask "what's the constraint?" before recommending a substitution
- Track substitutions in `logs/sessions/` so patterns emerge (e.g., if front squat substitutions consistently feel better, that might mean back squat needs technique work or a deload)
